## 4. What your marks actually predict about this exam

Your transcript is unusually informative here, because the RRB exam tests **almost none of what you
are good at.**

### 🔴 Numerical Ability — your documented weak spot, and the exam's heaviest section

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| 10th Mathematics | **45 / 100** |
| 11th Mathematics | **35 / 100** |
| 12th Mathematics | 68.40 / 100 |
| BCA — Algebra and Numerical Methods | **D (6)** |
| BCA — Probability and Statistics | C (7) |

Numerical Ability is **40 of 80 prelims marks** and **50 of 200 mains marks**. Your weakest
lifelong subject is half the qualifying exam. This is not a reason to quit — school arithmetic
speed is a trainable skill, not a talent — but it *is* the reason your plan must front-load it and
your daily tables/squares/fractions drill is non-negotiable.

### 🟡 Reasoning — middling, and it is the biggest single mains section

BCA Logical Reasoning and Thinking **B (8)**, Aptitude and Interpersonal Skills **C (7)**,
Aptitude and Workplace Skills **B (8)**, M.Sc. General Aptitude for Competitive Examinations
**B+**. Competent, not sharp. Reasoning is 40 marks in prelims and **50 in mains** — the
highest-weighted section of the exam. Puzzles and Seating Arrangement alone are 20–25 of those.

### 🟡 English — steady, needs maintenance, has a sectional cutoff

10th 52, 11th 54, 12th 72.62; General English I & II both **B**. Mains has 40 English marks with a
**minimum sectional score** you must clear — a weak English paper kills you outright regardless of
your total. Note: the mains language paper is **English or Hindi only**. Tamil is available as the
*medium* for the other four tests, but not as the language paper. So English it is.

### 🟢 Computer Knowledge — free marks, but only 20 of them

RDBMS **E (5)** and Java **D (6)** are your lowest grades — ignore that. The mains Computer section
is MS Office shortcuts, hardware, abbreviations, and generic networking. Your day job (FastAPI,
Python, Angular, NestJS, RAG systems) covers it many times over. But it is **20 marks, the smallest
section.** Cap your investment at ~15 hours. Do not let the one comfortable section eat your prep
time — that is the classic trap for technical candidates.

### ⚪ General Awareness — 40 marks, and you have no head start

No proxy in your transcript, because nobody's degree covers banking awareness and current affairs.
This is the section with the **highest return per hour invested**, and the one most often left too
late. 15 min/day starting now.

### The uncomfortable summary

Your A+ and O grades are in Deep Learning, Enterprise ML, Social Media & Text Analytics, AI, Data
Visualization, Project Work. **The RRB exam awards exactly zero marks for any of it.** Meanwhile
your two documented weaknesses — arithmetic speed and reasoning under time pressure — are worth
~90 of the 200 mains marks. The exam is close to orthogonal to your degree. Plan for the exam you
are sitting, not the one your transcript deserves.

### One genuinely encouraging signal

BCA GPA by semester: 7.61 → 7.41 → **6.30** → 7.57 → 7.78 → **8.67**.
M.Sc.: 8.10 → 8.58 → final CGPA **8.69, First Class with Distinction**, with a wall of A+/O across

You bottomed out in BCA Sem III and then climbed for six straight terms. That is documented

## 5. 🔄 This changes the Sep–Dec conflict — read this before you replan

[README.md §10](README.md) told you to pick between this exam and the coding plan, because both
want the same 2 hrs/day. **Your DOB changes that recommendation.**

At 22, you have roughly **six more Office Assistant cycles** (through ~2032) and **seven Scale-I
cycles**. IBPS RRB runs annually. There is no attempt limit in the notification. This exam is not a
now-or-never gate — it is an annual bus that stops at your corner every September for the next six
years.

So the rational play is **not** a 50/50 split, and **not** abandoning the coding plan:

1. **Apply for both posts before 21 Sep.** Cost: ~₹1,700 and about two hours of form-filling.
   Cheapest option you will ever have to keep the door open.
2. **Keep the coding plan as the primary owner of your 2 hrs/day.** It is the one with the harder
   deadline — it feeds your actual career at Krion and the MNC AI/GenAI target, and skills decay if
   you stop.
3. **Give this exam 30 focused minutes a day**, all of it on Numerical Ability speed drills — tables
   to 25, squares to 40, fractions↔percentages. That is the slowest-building, highest-leverage
   component, and the one that is useless to cram in November.
4. **Sit the November Scale-I prelims and the December OA prelims as scored baseline attempts.**
   Real exam hall, real pressure, real score. If you clear on 30 min/day, wonderful. If you don't,
   you now have a measured gap instead of a guess.
5. **Decide in January 2027, with data,** whether the 2027 cycle gets a full dedicated run.

This preserves both tracks honestly. The version that fails is the one where you pretend to do both
at full intensity, do neither, and have nothing to show in December.

## 7. Facts that carry over to your TNPSC goal

Your stated primary target is TNPSC Group IV. Two things in [madhesh.md](madhesh.md) matter there:

- **PSTM is marked "No" for every qualification.** In TNPSC, *Persons Studied in Tamil Medium* is a
  20% preferential reservation — a large advantage you will not have, since SSKV Boys Matric Hr Sec
  School and both your degrees were English medium. Your TNPSC competition is therefore against the
  general (non-PSTM) pool. Worth knowing before you build a TNPSC strategy around optimistic
  cutoffs. *(Verify against the actual TNPSC notification when it drops — PSTM rules are
  TNPSC-specific and are not covered by this IBPS notification.)*
- **Age 22 is early for the TNPSC ladder too**, which is an argument for using 2026–27 to build the
  shared foundation — arithmetic speed, reasoning, general studies — that Group IV, Group II/IIA,
  SSC CGL, RRB NTPC and bank exams all draw on. That foundation is the real asset. The specific
  exam you clear first matters less than you think.
