## 0. First, an honest word about "sure pass"

There is no plan that guarantees a pass. 244 Tamil Nadu posts will draw well over a lakh
applications. Anyone selling you certainty is selling you something.

What *does* exist is a plan that puts you in the top slice of candidates, and the truth about that
plan is boring: **it is not about hours, and it is not about how much you know.** Three things
decide it.

1. **Consistency beats intensity.** 2 hours × 90 days beats 8 hours × 20 days, every time. The
   people who fail are almost never the ones who studied too little in total — they are the ones
   who studied in bursts, went dark for ten days, and lost the arithmetic reflexes they'd built.
2. **Analysis beats attempts.** One mock analysed for 90 minutes is worth five mocks taken and
   never reviewed. Most candidates do the opposite, which is exactly why most candidates fail.
3. **Speed beats knowledge.** 80 questions in 45 minutes is **33 seconds per question.** Nothing in
   the prelims syllabus is intellectually hard for you. Every single mark you lose will be lost to
   the clock, not to ignorance. Train for the clock.

Your part of the bargain: **do not miss two days in a row.** Ever. One missed day is life. Two is
the beginning of the end. That single rule matters more than everything else in this file.

### The floor rule (this is what saves you)

Bad days will happen — a production incident, a late release, illness. On those days you do not
skip. You do the **45-minute floor**:

> **20 min speed drill + 15 min GA + 10 min of one topic you got wrong recently.**

The floor keeps the streak and keeps arithmetic reflexes warm. Skipping breaks both. There is a
gigantic psychological difference between "I did 45 minutes" and "I did nothing," and it compounds.

## Stage 3 — SPEED · Nov 9 – Dec 5 (4 weeks)

**Goal: stop learning, start performing.** No new topics from here. Everything is mocks, analysis,
and repair.

### The mock protocol — follow it exactly

1. **Take it like the real thing.** 45 minutes, one sitting, morning if possible, phone in another
   room. A mock taken with pauses teaches you nothing about the exam.
2. **Analyse for at least 60–90 minutes.** Longer than the mock itself. Non-negotiable.
3. **Label every wrong and every skipped question** with exactly one tag:

   | Tag | Meaning | The fix |
   |---|---|---|
   | **CARELESS** | Knew it, misread or miscalculated | Slow down 2 sec on reading. Usually the cheapest marks you'll ever recover. |
   | **DIDN'T KNOW** | Concept gap | Go relearn that topic this week |
   | **TOO SLOW** | Could have solved it with more time | You attempted too many. Cut attempts, raise accuracy. |
   | **BAD PICK** | Started a question you should have skipped | Selection discipline — see below |

4. **Count the tags.** Whichever dominates *is* your week's work. If it's TOO SLOW or BAD PICK, your
   problem is strategy, not knowledge, and more studying will not fix it.

### Question-selection discipline — the skill nobody teaches

In 45 minutes you cannot attempt all 80. Trying to is the most common way candidates fail.

- **Two passes.** Pass 1: every question you can finish in under 40 seconds. Pass 2: return to the
  heavy sets with what's left.
- **The 45-second rule.** If a question isn't yielding in 45 seconds, leave it. Not "one more try."
  Leave it. The sunk-cost instinct that says *I've already spent 30 seconds, I should finish* is
  what destroys papers.
- **Puzzles are all-or-nothing.** A 5-question seating set is 5 marks or 0. Scan the clues first: if
  it doesn't crack in 90 seconds, abandon it entirely and take a different set.
- **Negative marking is 0.25.** Wild guessing loses money. But if you can eliminate two of four
  options, the odds turn positive — guess then, not before.
- **Rough target split:** Reasoning ~30/40, Numerical ~25/40, accuracy above 85%. Look up Tamil
  Nadu's actual previous-year prelims cutoffs on the IBPS result page and adjust — *do not trust an
  unsourced cutoff number you read anywhere, including here.*

merit list. And there is a **minimum score in every one of the five sections** — a single failed
section ends it regardless of your total. You cannot abandon anything.

| **General Awareness** | 40 | **40** | ⚪ No head start — highest return per hour | **Most of your new time** |
| **English** | 40 | **40** | 🟡 Moderate, and it has a sectional cutoff | Second-most |
| Computer Knowledge | 40 | **20** | 🟢 Near-free for you | ~15 hours, then stop |

### Mains-specific strategy
- **Sectional time is fixed** — 30/15/15/30/30 min. You cannot borrow minutes from Computer to
  finish Numerical. Practise each section inside its own clock, not as one 120-minute block.
- **Computer is 15 minutes for 40 questions.** 22 seconds each. It is a recall sprint, not a
  reasoning test — which is exactly why your background makes it nearly free. Aim to finish early
  and bank the confidence, not the time (you can't transfer it).
- **GA is 15 minutes for 40 questions.** Also pure recall. You either know it or you don't; there is
  no working-out. This is precisely why it must be built from October, not January.
- **Insurance rule:** in your last four mocks, check that *every* section clears a plausible minimum.
  Your lowest section is your real score. Fix the floor, not the ceiling.

## 6. What failure actually looks like (so you can see it coming)

Almost nobody fails this exam by being unintelligent. They fail in one of four recognisable ways.

1. **The strong start.** Three brilliant weeks in September, work gets busy in October, ten days
   dark, and the arithmetic reflexes are gone. *Counter: the 45-minute floor. Never two days.*
2. **The collector.** Six YouTube channels, four books, three test series, endless Telegram groups.
   Feels productive, produces nothing. *Counter: one book per subject, one test series. Decide once.*
3. **The comfort trap.** Spends evenings on Computer Knowledge because it feels good, and avoids
   quant because it feels bad. **This is your specific risk** — Computer is 20 marks and Numerical
   is 90 across both papers. *Counter: quant goes in slot A, first thing, before your brain can
   negotiate.*
4. **The unanalysed mock.** Takes 40 mocks, reviews none, plateaus at the same score for two months
   and calls it bad luck. *Counter: 90 minutes of analysis per mock, error tags every time.*

If you recognise yourself in one of these mid-course, that recognition is the fix. Come back to this
section in November.
